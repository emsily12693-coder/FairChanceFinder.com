Fair chance support beyond the job search

Use these tools and support categories to prepare applications, gather documents, explain your background, and move toward stable work.

Preparation

Job Readiness

Prepare a short work-history explanation, practice interview answers, and keep a simple list of references who can confirm reliability, skills, attendance, and attitude.

  • Write a 30-second introduction focused on the job you want.
  • Prepare a brief, honest background explanation that ends with what has changed.
  • Save copies of certificates, training records, licenses, and volunteer work.

Legal next steps

Record Support

Review whether you may qualify for expungement, set-aside, sealing, restoration of rights, or a certificate of rehabilitation. Rules vary by state and charge, so use official court or legal-aid guidance before paying a private service.

  • Request a copy of your own background report so you know what employers may see.
  • Check court records for old warrants, unpaid fines, or case-status errors.
  • Keep proof of completed probation, treatment, classes, or restitution.

Application basics

Documents Checklist

Having documents ready helps you apply faster when an employer is ready to move.

  • Government ID, Social Security card, birth certificate, or work authorization documents.
  • Resume, reference list, certificates, licenses, and training records.
  • Transportation plan, availability schedule, and preferred contact information.

Wraparound support

Stability Support

Work searches are easier when immediate barriers are handled. Track local programs that help with clothing, transportation, food, housing, phones, treatment, and counseling.

  • Ask workforce centers about bus passes, interview clothing, and training vouchers.
  • Use community reentry programs for case management and referrals.
  • Keep a written weekly schedule for applications, follow-ups, and interviews.

Fair hiring

Fair Hiring Rights

Many employers must follow rules when using background checks. In general, candidates should receive notice, a chance to review reports, and a chance to correct inaccurate information.

  • Ask what background-check company is being used and request your copy.
  • Respond quickly if an employer asks for context or documentation.
  • Keep explanations factual, brief, and connected to the role.
