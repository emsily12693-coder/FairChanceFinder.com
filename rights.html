Know Your Rights

Understanding fair-chance hiring laws in Arizona can help you navigate the job search with confidence.

Key takeaway: In Arizona, many employers cannot ask about your criminal history on an initial job application. You have the right to be evaluated on your qualifications first.

Ban the Box in Arizona

Arizona was one of the first states to adopt "Ban the Box" policies for public employment. Here's what this means for you:

  • State agencies cannot ask about criminal history on initial job applications.
  • Many cities (including Phoenix, Tucson, and others) have extended this to city employment.
  • Background checks typically cannot be run until after a conditional job offer is made.
  • Private employers are increasingly adopting fair-chance practices voluntarily.

Arizona HB 2617 — Fair Chance Hiring

Arizona House Bill 2617 provides additional protections for job seekers with criminal records:

  • Limits when employers can inquire about criminal history during the hiring process.
  • Requires employers to consider the nature, severity, and recency of offenses.
  • Employers must weigh whether the offense is directly related to the job duties.
  • Provides a pathway for individuals to explain their circumstances.

What Employers Can and Cannot Do

Employers CANNOT:

Use an arrest record that did not result in a conviction to deny you a job in most circumstances. Older offenses may also be limited from consideration depending on the type of role.

Employers MUST:

Provide you with a copy of the background check report if it is used as a basis for an adverse employment decision. You have the right to dispute inaccurate information.

You Have the Right To:

Explain your record and provide context. Many employers are required to conduct an individualized assessment rather than applying blanket policies that exclude all applicants with records.

Setting Aside a Conviction in Arizona

Arizona allows you to apply to "set aside" certain convictions after completing your sentence. While this does not erase the record, it shows the court has recognized your rehabilitation. Benefits include:

  • A court order stating the conviction has been set aside.
  • Improved chances in job applications and housing.
  • Restoration of certain civil rights (like the right to vote and own firearms, depending on the offense).
  • Free legal clinics are available to help with the application process.

Tips for Your Job Search

  • Be honest but strategic — You don't have to volunteer your history upfront, but never lie on an application.
  • Prepare your explanation — Practice a brief, positive statement about what you've learned and how you've grown.
  • Know your timeline — Older convictions carry less weight. Know how long ago your offense was.
  • Target fair-chance employers — Companies listed on FairChance Finder have committed to fair-chance hiring practices.
  • Get your record reviewed — A legal aid attorney can help you understand exactly what shows up and whether anything can be set aside.
